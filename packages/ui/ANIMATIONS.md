# Animation spec

Animations are features of the show, so each one is designed here before it's coded. Every entry lists what triggers it, what moves, its duration and easing, any sound, the reduced-motion fallback, and the cost budget. The code refers to each animation by its **hook name**: `data-anim="…"` on the element, a `--dur-*` token for its length, and on the projector a `quina:anim` event with `detail.name`. A theme can then restyle the motion without touching logic.

Status: a draft for the user's review (2026-11-24). Numbers are starting points, to be tuned at the rehearsal.

## Principles

1. **One dominant motion at a time.** The projector is seen from far away, so only one big thing moves at once. The drum owns the Spin, the title owns the Landing, and the card owns the Check. Everything else stays still or idles very gently.
2. **Motion never tells a secret.** Nothing moves *before* a song lands:
   - no anticipation of the title;
   - no near-win pulse timed to songs;
   - Heat never changes at a landing (`packages/rules/heat.ts` delays and eases it).
   A phone's drum shows only songs still to come, blurred while it turns and in an order that has nothing to do with the Queue, so it can't hint at the next song (the user's canvas comment of 25 Nov: real covers on every drum tile).
3. **Timed by the show's clock, not by when a message arrives.** Moments with a timestamp (`landAtMs`, a revealed song's `atMs`, `shuffle.startAtMs`/`endAtMs`, `spin.startAtMs`) start at that time on the sender's clock, corrected with `ClockOffset`. So the projector, the console and the phones agree, and a late message starts mid-animation instead of late.
4. **Sound belongs to whoever owns the speaker:**
   - the engine plays everything on the PA;
   - the projector is silent;
   - the console plays only soft touch sounds, for its own taps: on by default, switchable off in its menu (U182, which replaced "the console is silent"), and checked at the night's sound check so the presenters' microphones don't pick them up;
   - phones play only short interface sounds: touch sounds and game cues (see "Sound map");
   - a page on the show laptop is silent whatever its setting, since that output is the PA (D207).
5. **Reduced motion** (`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, and on phones also Data Saver): every animation has a still fallback that keeps the information (D209):
   - Moving and looping animations (anything that slides, scales, turns, shakes, blinks or loops) are declared only under `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`, so the still state is the default. On phones they also need `data-motion='full'`, which the app sets from both the media query and Data Saver.
   - Colour and opacity fades that carry meaning run either way, with their usual timing: a connection's colour, a switch's track, the backdrops, the default action's ring, a crossfade between two views.
   - The fallbacks that show something else (a percentage for a sweep, a static outline for a ring, the Shuffle's caption) live in `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce)` blocks.
   - The "Reduced motion" columns below say what remains.
6. **Budgets:**
   - Animate only `transform` and `opacity` (and `filter` on the projector only).
   - Never animate layout properties. One documented exception: the console's `finale-button` grows its row (`grid-template-rows`, 250 ms), as the spec asks; it runs once per threshold, on the tablet only.
   - Animations stop while the page is hidden.
   - **Phones** (the target is a 4-year-old mid-range Android): at most 24 elements animating at once; no animated blur or backdrop filters; under 4 ms of JS per frame. Two static exceptions, the user's (D132's boards): the corner buttons' backdrop blur, and the drum's covers blurred while it turns (set once when the Spin starts and lifted at the landing, never animated).
   - **Projector** (the show laptop at 60 fps, sharing the CPU with the audio engine): at most 150 animated elements; keep the main thread free around `landAtMs`, with no layout work in the 300 ms before a landing.
7. **Easing** (`packages/ui/tokens.css`):
   - `--ease-out` is the plain deceleration for slides and fades.
   - `--ease-bounce` is a real spring, drawn with CSS `linear()`. It starts fast, overshoots to 1.11 at about 35 % of the time, swings back once to 0.99 and settles by the end. Every "bounce", "pop", "slam" and "stamp" below uses it, so the overshoot of a move is about 11 % of its distance: scale 2 → 1 dips to 0.89; 0.4 → 1 peaks at 1.07.
   - Keyframes don't fake an overshoot with extra stops. The timing function does it. Stops stay only where the motion itself has steps, such as a squash or a pop that settles somewhere else.
   - `--ease-kick` is the same spring let go with a push: it shoots past the end to 1.66 of the distance at about 17 % of the time, dips once to 0.93 and settles. Only `corner-press` uses it, so a 6 % dip springs back to 1.04.

## The garland's lights (projector and phones)

The user's canvas comment #84 (D-t): the bulbs light up in patterns, each bulb driven on its own; more colours for a win and for a phone's own song; fire when the Heat is very hot. One pure module picks the pattern and each bulb's part in it (`packages/ui/motion/garland.ts`: the wire and bulbs' layout, `garlandLights` and `garlandBulbs`); each app draws it with CSS keyframes, and each bulb's place in the pattern is a custom property on it (its index, its step of three, a random seed), never per-frame JS.

**Anatomy.** A wire in swags; each bulb hangs from it, tilted by half the wire's slope: an ink cap, the glass (its dark unlit colour), the **lamp** over the glass (the lit colour inside the outline, with its halo under it) and the ink outline on top. Only the lamp moves: its opacity is the bulb's light, so a dim lamp lets the dark glass show and a lamp at 0 is a bulb switched off. The halo's size and strength follow the Heat (4 + 22 × Heat, as S2) and don't move.

**Colour.** The Heat patterns keep the three tones along the string, mixed cool (ice) → warm (gold, orange) → hot (red, orange, gold) as the Heat rises, so the colour always says the Heat. `party` and `win` swap them for six party colours in order along the string (red, orange, gold, green, ice, lilac); their halos keep the Heat's size and their pace and flames still follow its level, so the Heat stays readable.

The new pattern takes over at once; its lamps are already between 12 and 100 %, so it reads as the string switching program, like real fairy lights.

**Budget.** Phones: one animated element per lamp in the per-bulb patterns (13, and 4 flames: 17 at most); `party` animates the 3 groups instead of the lamps (3, and 4 flames), so with `your-song`'s 13 loops the phone stays at 20, within its 24. Opacity and transform only; the halos and flames are gradients, never an animated blur or shadow. The garland pauses while the page is hidden and while the Celebration's screen covers it; offline nothing runs. Projector: 34 lamps and 17 flames, 51 at most, within its 150; a hidden scene's garland doesn't run.

**Reduced motion** (and Data Saver on phones): nothing loops or flickers, and the still garland still shows the Heat: the tones and glow size of the value; when cool, every 4th bulb at 60 % (S2's look); when very hot, the flames drawn still; the party colours for `your-song` and `win`. The colour changes as a fade (the projector's 2 s).

**Beat sync** (optional in #84): not now. Neither the public feed nor the projector's events carry the playing song's beat (the projector's `transition.scheduled` has a `bpm` but no beat phase, and publishing a tempo before the landing would hint at the song). If Sound engine publishes, per landing and only from its `landAtMs`, a beat grid (`bpm`, the epoch ms of one downbeat on the sender's clock, and beats per bar), the patterns could set their period to one or two bars and their phase from that downbeat, with `ClockOffset`, and drop back to these tempo-free periods when a song has no stable tempo.
