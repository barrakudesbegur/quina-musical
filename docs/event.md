# Quina Musical: the event

This page explains the event: what happens on the night, who does what, and how Barrakudes wants it to run. It says nothing about how the software is built. The system requirements come from this page, and the software comes from the requirements.

- Numbers from past editions are examples, not rules. Each edition sets its own.
- **New in 2026** marks something the team hasn't tried yet.
- Terms follow [GLOSSARY.md](../GLOSSARY.md). Words in quotes are what people say or see, in Catalan.

## In short

- Quina Musical is a music bingo night in Begur, held every year around 27 December.
- Players get printed cards with 12 songs instead of numbers. Songs play one after another, and players cover each song on their card with a corn seed.
- The first card with a full line wins the Line. The first full card wins the round: players shout "Quina!".
- Two presenters host the night, with DJ-style music and a big screen. It's a Christmas party for the whole town, not just a game.

## Barrakudes and the team

- **Barrakudes** is a group of young people from Begur who want to get young people active and involved in village life by organizing events and parties. They're volunteers.
- The Quina is their own event: they prepare it, run it and clean up. The first edition was in 2024. 2026 is the third.
- **Roles on the night:**
  - **The presenters:** two members on stage all night, each with a microphone.
  - **The bar:** drinks, snacks and the cards. At the start one to three people work the till. Later, whoever is free serves whoever comes to the bar.
  - **The tech** ("el tècnic"): one member who builds the software, prepares the songs and the cards, and runs the laptop. They sit near the laptop to fix problems and help the presenters.
  - **The others:** they sometimes walk between the tables to sell more cards, food or drinks.
- **How we work:**
  - We prepare over several weeks, together.
  - Everyone comes to set up and to take down, because that's where most of the effort goes. We keep the whole day of the event free.
  - Organizers wear something that marks them as the team, such as Christmas hats or the same sweatshirt.
  - After each edition we write a retrospective.
- **New things are hard to introduce.** The organizers and presenters are slow to adopt new tools. Anything new must be easy, rehearsed, and backed by a simple fallback to the old way.

## Money

- **Entry is free.**
- **Our income:** the bar (drinks and snacks) and the cards.
  - 2025 bar prices: water 1.50 €, soft drinks 2 €, beer 2.50 €, mixed drinks 5–6 €, crisps, popcorn and nuts 1–1.50 €, hot chocolate 2 €, a slice of pizza 3 €.
- **The bar takes cash and card.** It has two card terminals and a cash box in the middle. A token system (buy tokens, swap them for drinks) is only used at big parties.
- **Sponsors** give prizes for free, and some give a small amount of money (for example 50 €). We spend it, and our own money, on items for the prize hampers.
- **Licences:** the association holds the licences needed to play recorded music in public.

## When and where

- **The date:** every year around 27 December. This year it's **Sunday 27 December 2026**.
- **Times:**
  - The plan: card sales from 17:30, the first round at 18:00.
  - The rounds last until about 22:00, then a DJ set closes the night.
  - 2025: the Game started at 18:13, the first song landed at 18:19, and the last round ended at 21:55; the DJ played until 23:00.
  - 2024: the start slipped to 18:30 because the lights failed, and the rounds ended at 22:00.
- **The venue:** a large pop-up tent on a town square (2025: Plaça Esteva i Cruañas).
  - It's cold, so we ask for heaters.
  - The town council lends:
    - tables and chairs for about 300 people;
    - bars, fridges and tents;
    - power, extension leads and bins;
    - two speakers and two microphones.
- **Internet:** there's no Wi-Fi, but there's good 5G mobile data. Phones can share a hotspot. A router isn't an option.
- **Sound:** two big speakers on stage and a simple mixing desk. There's no sound team.
  - In 2024 the sound was too quiet at the back. We want a stronger system and a sound check beforehand.
- **Projection:** one 4:3 projector, far from the audience, so only big things are readable. A 16:9 projector might replace it.
- **Microphones:** both wired and wireless are available. The choice isn't fixed.
- **Show equipment:**
  - the laptop, which drives the projector and the music, in the first row or at the side of the stage;
  - one tablet shared by the presenters (**new in 2026**);
  - the tech's phone;
  - a Wii remote for the presenters.

## The audience

- **The whole town comes:** families, young people and grandparents. Many come back every year.
- **About 200–300 people.** In 2024 the roughly 200 chairs weren't enough.
- Most people buy several cards and also follow their relatives' cards.
- **Many people distrust computers,** and many barely use their phones. Everything must feel familiar and simple.
- **Everything is in Catalan,** in informal, natural language.

## The cards

- **A card** has 12 songs in 3 lines of 4, a number (written "#123") and a QR code that opens the player's phone page. Three cards print on each page.
- **Card types:**
  - **normal cards** play the regular rounds;
  - **special cards** play the Quina Especial. Its prizes are bigger, so its card is sold separately.
  - In 2025 the special card also played the Quina del Desgraciat: the price sheet at the bar listed it for "Quina especial i quina del desgraciat".
  - The types are fixed when the cards are printed, with fewer special cards than normal ones. Each type has its own colour.
- **2026: the normal card is printed on both sides,** with the same number. Side 1 plays Quines 1–5 and side 2 plays Quines 6–10, each with its own songs. Side 2 has its own design, and each side shows which rounds it's for. The presenters explain it in rounds 1 and 6 and keep reminding people during the round.
- **Each edition** has a new title (the paper colour may stay), so last year's cards are easy to spot and don't count. Card numbers restart each edition, normal cards first (2025: normal #1–399, special #400–699).
- **Corn seeds** are the markers: they're compostable, cheap and don't slide much. They come off easily, so one normal card serves every regular round.
- **Printing:**
  - The cards are generated from the final song list, so no two cards or lines are the same.
  - Once they're printed, no song can change.
  - Print well ahead of time. In 2024 we printed the day before, and that mustn't happen again.
  - Printing itself takes a few hours, at a local print shop.
- **Prices:**
  - The price isn't printed on the card; a price list hangs at the bar.
  - A normal card's price drops as rounds are played. In 2025 it was 1.50 € for each regular round still to play: 15 € before the first round, 13.50 € after one, down to 1.50 € for the last. The Desgraciat doesn't count.
  - A special card costs a fixed price (2025: 5 €).
- **Sales:**
  - Cards are sold only on the night, never before: at the bar, in cash or by card. There's a long queue at the start.
  - During the night, members also sell between the tables. In 2024, going round to sell special cards before the Quina Especial worked very well.
- **Knowing which cards are sold:**
  - Cards are printed in stacks of 50. Sellers take cards in order, from one or two stacks, so the sold numbers form ranges.
  - The tech updates the ranges during the night. It's reliable, but not exact: a range can contain unsold cards.
- **Amounts:**
  - 2024: 250 of each type printed; 214 normal and 147 special sold.
  - 2025: about 250 cards in play expected; about 190 normal (#1 to about #190) and 151 special (#400–550) actually played.

## The songs

- **The pool** is the curated playlist the cards are printed from.
  - Over several weeks, everyone in the team adds songs to a shared Spotify playlist.
  - Then a few members meet and filter it down to the final count.
  - **2026 has two pools of 70 songs each,** with different songs: one for Quines 1–5 and one for Quines 6–10. The first was final on 25 November; the second is still being filled. The Especial and the Desgraciat may get a third.
  - An archive playlist keeps past songs.
- **The size is a hard choice** because it shapes the night: more songs mean longer rounds. 2024 had 87 songs, which was too many. 2025 had 77 (the plan said about 80, and fewer was fine). Each edition decides its own.
- **The songs:** popular hits from every era and genre, including Catalan, Spanish and Christmas songs.
- **Every round plays a shuffle of its whole pool.** A round ends long before the pool runs out: in 2025 about 48 of 77 songs.
- **Every song starts at its hook,** its most recognizable moment, usually a chorus.
  - Before the software, songs were cut by hand, which was tedious.
  - Now the tech prepares them.
- **No skill needed:** the presenters say each song's name out loud, and the screen and phones show it.
  - Players who recognize a song early feel clever, which is fun, but it gives them no advantage.
- **The songs are never published.**

## The night, step by step

1. **Set-up:** in the afternoon, with everyone, including a sound check.
2. **Doors and card sales (from 17:30):**
   - music plays and the bar opens;
   - the screen shows the night's rounds, the card types with their colours and prices, the QR code for phones, and the sponsors.
3. **The welcome:** the presenters explain the game.
   - They cover the number of rounds, the Desgraciat, which round is the Especial, the throws, and how winners are identified and collect their prizes. They remind everyone about the bar.
   - There are no written instructions anywhere.
4. **The rounds** (see below), with an Interval between each two.
5. **The throws:** between some rounds, a break of about 5 minutes ("parón").
   - Organizers throw things to the crowd, such as fuets, sweets or chocolates, so people get up off their chairs and the night doesn't drag.
   - The presenters put on a bit of a show or go round the tables talking to people.
6. **The close:**
   - the presenters explain how to collect prizes and give a short closing speech;
   - the sponsors are thanked;
   - a DJ set follows.
7. **Take-down,** with everyone.

**Rounds per night:**
- 2024: 10 normal and 2 special. The night felt long.
- 2025: 10 regular rounds, then the Desgraciat, then the Especial: 12 rounds.
- Each edition decides its own. **The order can change during the night**, for example to move the Desgraciat.

## One round

1. **The intro:** the presenters present the round ("Quina 3") and announce its prizes and sponsors. Only now are that round's prizes revealed.
   - A round never starts by itself, because announcing the prizes takes as long as it takes.
2. **The Shuffle:**
   - the round's song order is decided at random, in public, at the start of the round;
   - all the songs show in a grid and get shuffled into the drum;
   - the order never changes afterwards, and nobody can pick songs.
3. **The songs land one after another** (**new in 2026**).
   - **The drum:** a reel of album covers spins on the screen and stops on the next song exactly as its hook hits. That moment is the **landing**, and from then the song counts.
   - **The music:** it's mixed like a real DJ set. Transitions are quick and light early in a round, and grow bigger and more suspenseful as a win gets closer.
   - **The pace:**
     - songs come slower in the first round of the night, when every song is new (about 22 s each), and faster afterwards (about 14 s);
     - by default the songs advance by themselves, and the presenters can take over at any time;
     - near the end of a round the presenters advance by hand.
4. **The Line:** the first card with a complete line of 4 wins the Line prize. There's only one Line per round. The round goes on.
5. **The Bingo:** the first full card, with all 12 songs, wins the round's main prize. Players shout "Quina!". **The round ends.**
6. **The Celebration,** then the **Interval:** relaxed music while the organizers note the winners and reset. The next round's prizes stay hidden until its intro.

## Claims and checks

- **Players must claim.** They shout "Línia!" or "Quina!". Nobody ever tells a player they have won.
- **On a claim:**
  - the presenters stop new songs, but the music keeps going quietly underneath;
  - a presenter goes to the player with the microphone, or the player comes forward;
  - they take the card number.
- **The Check is public** (**new in 2026**).
  - The card appears on the screen, and its songs are checked one by one as landed or not.
  - A Line check shows the line; a Bingo check shows all 12 songs.
  - Until 2025, one presenter read the songs out while the other pretended to check, and the tech really checked the number on the laptop. Some people felt it wasn't fair.
- **A card is invalid** when its number doesn't exist, or when it's the wrong type for the round.
- **A card not registered as sold** only raises a warning. The presenters ask to see the card.
- **Forged cards:** we assume nobody forges cards. If two cards claim the same win, the fake one is easy to tell apart.
- **The Check's result decides.**
  - In a strange case, the presenters can still reject a card that passed.
  - They can never accept a card that failed.
- **Ties:**
  - the first to claim wins;
  - claims on the same song share the prize, and the winners agree how to split it;
  - the Shuffle makes sure the first Line and the first Bingo can never be shared by two cards in play. That's a public rule. A tie can still happen through a card that isn't registered as sold.
- **Late claims:** a player who completed earlier but claims late still wins if nobody else has claimed. Any other late case is decided by the team on the spot.
- **An unclaimed win is lost.** Nobody goes looking for winners.
- **Going back:** if a claim comes after more songs have played, the presenters can undo those songs so they don't count. Players simply lift the corn seeds off.
- **Several claims at once:** each card is checked before the round moves on.
- **A failed check** gets a funny "fail" moment, with video-game music while the presenters sort it out. Then the round goes on.
- **A passed check** gets a Celebration with festive music. The winner is noted: their name, or a numbered slip we hand them. They collect the prize later.

## The special rounds

- **The Quina del Desgraciat,** once a night:
  - It's played in reverse. Everyone with a card stands up. When a song on your card lands, you sit down.
  - **The last one standing wins.** If the last players standing all sit on the same song, they share the win.
  - There's no Line.
  - Some cards in a sold range may not have been sold, so the presenters call the winner by looking at the room.
  - It's short (2025: 21 songs, about 8 minutes), lively and full of interaction with the crowd.
- **The Quina Especial,** usually the last round:
  - only special cards play;
  - its prizes are the biggest of the night (2025: a TV);
  - 2024 had two special rounds.

## Prizes and sponsors

- **Hampers ("paneres"):** each round's Bingo wins a hamper (2025: 12 hampers for 12 rounds). A hamper has:
  - one headline item: a ham, or an appliance such as a coffee maker, an air fryer, earbuds, a toaster or a griddle, and a TV for the Especial;
  - Christmas staples: chocolate, panettone, turrón, neules, wine, cava, fruit, rice, tomatoes, fuet and a Barrakudes T-shirt;
  - vouchers and products from several sponsors.
- **The Line's prize:** something small. 2024: a bottle of white wine, a fuet, neules and a small sponsor prize.
- **Sponsors:** local businesses such as restaurants, food shops, pharmacies, shops, estate agents, hotels and services.
  - They give products, vouchers or money. Barrakudes also gives some prizes.
  - 2024: 34 sponsors. 2025: about 55.
- **Tracking:** the organizers keep a spreadsheet with each sponsor's category, what they give, the logo, whether it was delivered, who holds it, pending tasks (some prizes are picked up on the day itself) and which Instagram post features them.
- **Instagram:** before the night we publish the poster and several posts thanking the sponsors (2025: 6). In 2024 everything went up at the last minute; we want to start earlier.
- **Assigning prizes to rounds:** about 3–4 days before the night. A sponsor may still bring a prize on the day, so the assignment can change during the night.
- **The reveal:**
  - a round's prizes are shown only at its intro;
  - round 1's are visible before the night, and later rounds stay locked;
  - the organizers can choose to keep everything secret.
- **Mention everyone:**
  - before each round the presenters read out every prize and its sponsor, including sponsors who gave money or appliances;
  - they keep the prizes alive during the round ("a veure qui s'emportarà…");
  - in 2024 some sponsors were forgotten.
- **The presenters' cheat sheet,** printed and on the tablet: the prizes and sponsors to read out.
- **No prize ceremony:** big prizes aren't handed over on stage. Winners collect them when they leave or at the end, by showing their card or slip.

## The presenters

- **Who they are:**
  - two members on stage all night, each with their own style;
  - they're free to improvise: going down to the tables, asking people questions, joking with the crowd.
  - In 2024 we learned that the microphone role matters more than anything. It needs preparation, rehearsal and energy, especially towards the front rows.
- **Their jobs:**
  - welcome everyone and explain the game;
  - announce each round's prizes and sponsors;
  - say each song's name right on its landing;
  - handle claims and checks;
  - hype the crowd and run the throws;
  - remind people about the bar;
  - close the night.
- **The moment to keep:** in 2025 a presenter got the crowd chanting "eh, eh, eh!" just before the next song.
  - The fun came from controlling the exact moment the song changed.
  - The new show must keep that control and create moments like it.
- **What they can do at any time** (**new in 2026**):
  - **"Següent":** the next song now;
  - **"Parlem":** talk over the music. No new song lands, and the music goes down;
  - **"Pausa":** a long break mid-round. No song lands, the relaxed music plays, and the screen and phones show "Tornem de seguida";
  - **"Aguanta":** keep the current song at full volume, for dancing or a crowd moment;
  - **"Suspens":** the current song keeps playing while the tension builds, and the drum hovers over a few songs. On release, the real next song lands;
  - **"Enrere":** undo the last song, so it doesn't count;
  - **check a card;**
  - **sound effects,** such as a horn or a fanfare;
  - **change the pace,** or switch automatic advance off and go by hand.
- **What they see privately:**
  - the next song, so they can name it on time;
  - the prizes to announce;
  - when the round and the night should end, and how far ahead or behind they are;
  - on request, how close cards are to winning and who might win. It's hidden by default, so the presenters stay intrigued too.
- **Their equipment:**
  - **The shared tablet** (**new in 2026**). The presenters may not like it. Then the tech drives the show from the laptop, and they advance by hand.
  - **The Wii remote** is the intended experience. It's fun, the presenters joke about it, and shaking it can start a Suspense. It needs a plan B: the tablet can do everything the remote does. In 2025 its range from the stage to the laptop was at the limit.
- **The tech** can open the presenters' controls on a phone to help them.

## Fairness and trust

- **Why it matters:** many people distrust computers. The game must look fair and feel familiar.
- **A public shuffle:** each round's order is set at its start, in front of everyone, and never changes.
- **The drum is what people trust:** it spins visibly and stops on each song, like a real draw. No code or proof is shown on screen. Showing one wouldn't prove anything.
- **The Check is public:** everyone sees the card checked song by song.
- **Only landings count:**
  - a bit of a song heard before it lands, as a tease or a decoy, never counts;
  - a tease or decoy must never feel as if it counted, and must always resolve clearly.
- **The ending stays secret:** nothing public shows the next song, the order, who might win, or how close the end is. Tension can show on screen, with some randomness, so it never gives the end away.
- **Open code:** the software is public. If someone doubts it, we suggest they ask an AI to check the code. It's a town bingo, not a high-stakes contest.

## Players' phones

- **Optional:** the game works fully with paper and corn. Phones are a companion.
- **Getting in:** players scan the QR on their card. There's nothing to install and no account.
  - They can add more cards by scanning or by typing the number.
  - They can name each card, for example "Àvia".
- **What the phone shows:**
  - the current song and the drum;
  - the current round's songs so far (no earlier rounds);
  - their cards, with every landed song marked;
  - a gentle hint when a card is one song away;
  - the prizes revealed so far;
  - the night's schedule.
- **Never:** the phone never says "you won". Players must notice and shout. Once the presenters confirm a win, only the winner's own phone celebrates it.
- **The grandmother test:**
  - large, clear and simple, in Catalan;
  - sounds that can be muted;
  - the screen stays on;
  - it reconnects by itself.
- **Each edition starts clean:** last year's data and links on phones are cleared, and an old card shows that it's from last year.

## Atmosphere and style

- **A show, not a meeting:** a festive Christmas look, styled like a mobile game, "juicy" and full of animation on the screen and phones. Animations are part of the show and are planned like any other feature.
- **Music like a real DJ** (**new in 2026**):
  - several layers at once, matched tempos, changes on the musical phrase;
  - teases of the next song, and real DJ tricks;
  - never robotic, repetitive or a plain volume fade.
- **Intensity grows** towards each Line and Bingo, and drops back after a Line.
- **The music never stops,** unless the silence is intended: during claims, pauses and Intervals too.
- **Fun sounds:**
  - a game-style shuffle sound;
  - video-game music while a failed check is sorted out;
  - fanfares for a pass or a fail;
  - a "final lap" warning as a win gets close;
  - the presenters' sound effects.
- **Mascots:** Barrakudes' figures ("ninos") may appear, only family-friendly ones, and only if they fit the style.

## Timing

- **The goals:**
  - start on time;
  - steady rounds;
  - end on time;
  - know at any moment when the round and the night will end, and how far ahead or behind we are.
- **The pace catches up gently** when we're late, never during the tense final moments of a round.
- **The Intervals and checks** count in the plan.
- **2025 in numbers:**
  - regular rounds averaged 16 minutes (11–20) and 50 songs (42–56); the Desgraciat took 21;
  - the first round was the slowest;
  - Intervals averaged 2.5 minutes (1–5.5);
  - a Line check took 1–2 minutes, and a Bingo check plus the round's end 1.5–3.5 minutes.
  - The plan had been 12–14 minutes a round at about 15 s a song.
- **2024:** we expected 35 songs a round and got about 60. Nobody knew when the night would end.
- **Rehearsals:** full-show rehearsals on days before the night, never on the day itself.

## Before the night

- **Weeks before:**
  - build the playlist;
  - find sponsors among Begur's businesses;
  - buy the hamper items and baskets;
  - file the town council request: tent, heaters, furniture, power, bins, speakers, microphones;
  - publish the poster with a clear date, then the sponsor posts.
- **After the playlist is final:** generate the cards and print them well ahead.
- **3–4 days before:** assign the prizes to rounds and build the hampers.
- **Days before:** rehearsals, including the presenters.
- **On the day:**
  - pick up the last prizes;
  - set up with everyone;
  - check the sound;
  - stock plenty of food. In 2024 the snacks ran short, and with no dinner on offer people left the DJ set early.

## What must never fail

- **The internet may drop:** the show must never stop because of it, and the music never depends on it.
- **Equipment can fail:** if the laptop dies, another one must resume the night at the same round and song.
- **People may not adopt the new tools:** every new way of working falls back to the old one. The tech drives, the presenters advance by hand, and the tablet can stand in for the Wii remote.
- **Privacy:** winners' names are never shown on screen or published.
- **The venue:** it's cold, the lights can fail (2024), and the sound can be too quiet at the back.
