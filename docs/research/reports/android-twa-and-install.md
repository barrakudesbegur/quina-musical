# Android: a Play Store app (TWA), the QR, and the PWA install button

Research report, 27 Nov 2026. Sources were read on that day; dates are the sources' own dates where they give one. Chrome stable is 158 (released 17 Nov 2026).

## Verdicts

**1. A TWA on Google Play: pass.** A TWA is just our website shown full screen by Chrome inside a tiny Android app. It gives us a Play Store listing and a "real app" icon, and nothing else the players need: the installed PWA already gets an app icon, full screen, a splash screen and its own entry in Android's app list. The cost doesn't fit the calendar. A personal developer account created after 13 Nov 2023 must run a closed test with 12 testers for 14 days in a row, then wait up to 7 days for production access, then wait for the app's review (up to 7 days or more for some accounts). Starting today, the earliest public release is around 18–25 Dec, with no slack for a rejection. Bare web-wrapper apps are also the classic Play rejection. For grandma, "go to the Play Store, sign in, install" is more steps than "scan, it opens". The paper card already works without any phone. Revisit only if the account is an organisation account or an old personal account, and then only as a nice-to-have.

**2. Temporary availability: possible, but nothing is truly temporary.** Play now lets you **unpublish** an app (new users can't find or install it; existing users keep it and still get updates) and, since 2025, **delete** it from the console if it's unpublished and on fewer than 1,000 active devices. Deletion can be undone for 7 days. After that the app is gone for good, and its package name can never be used again. Copies already on phones stay there until their owners remove them, and they keep opening our website. The test tracks don't help for a public event: internal tests take at most 100 testers, closed tests need testers' emails or a Google Group, and open testing stays locked until production access is granted. "Private apps" are for an organisation's own managed devices, not the public. Deleting an app has no documented effect on the developer account. The account can be closed only for inactivity (more than a year old with no app ever submitted, or 180 days without using the console plus unverified contacts), and this account is used anyway.

**3. What the QR opens on Android: plan for the browser.** The camera app (or the Quick Settings QR tile) hands the link to Android. If an installed app has a verified claim on the address, that app opens. Otherwise the default browser opens. An installed PWA (a "WebAPK" on Chrome) does register its scope with Android, so **if it's installed, a camera scan usually opens it directly**. Android has done this for years; it isn't a new Chrome feature and there's no version to wait for. But it isn't guaranteed: Google Lens inside the Google app often opens links in its own in-app browser, which skips the hand-off, and a 2026 Chromium bug report shows WebAPK link claims sometimes dropping to "not verified". A TWA with verified App Links has the same two weak spots. The grandma-proof path is the one that always works: **the browser page must be the complete experience** (card shown, songs marked, the win). Opening in the installed app is a bonus. Whatever opens, `/26/<number>` must add or show that card.

**4. Our own install button: yes, Chrome-only, as a small optional extra.** Listen for `beforeinstallprompt`, call `preventDefault()` (this hides Chrome's own mini-infobar), keep the event, and show our button only while we hold one. On tap, call `prompt()`, which works once per event. Chrome fires the event only when the app isn't installed and the user has tapped the page at least once and spent 30 seconds on it, so the button never appears at first load. The service worker is no longer needed for installing from the menu, and current Chrome reportedly fires the event without one too. Detect "already installed" with `matchMedia('(display-mode: standalone)')` (we're running as the app) and `navigator.getInstalledRelatedApps()` with a `webapp` entry (Android Chrome 84+, called from inside the scope: the PWA is installed but this is a browser tab). Then we can say "Obre l'app" instead of "Instal·la". `appinstalled` tells us when it worked. New in 2026: `navigator.install()` and the `<install>` element shipped in Chrome 158, **on desktop only**; Android support is announced for later, so don't plan on it for 27 Dec. Firefox and Samsung-without-Chrome users get no event; iPhones never get one (they install by hand from the Share menu).

## Key facts and sources

### TWA and Play

- New personal accounts (created after 13 Nov 2023): a closed test with ≥12 testers opted in continuously for ≥14 days before applying for production; the application review "usually takes 7 days or less"; Production and pre-registration stay locked until then; open testing becomes available only after production access. Organisation accounts are exempt (they need a D-U-N-S number). [Play Console Help: App testing requirements for new personal developer accounts](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14151465) (read 27 Nov 2026; third-party guides from late 2026 confirm the rule is unchanged).
- Review times: "For certain developer accounts, we'll take more time… review times of up to seven days or longer in exceptional cases." [Play Console Help: Publish your app](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859751).
- Spam policy: no apps whose primary purpose is a webview of a website without the owner's permission (ours would be allowed since we own the site); minimum functionality is a separate policy. [Play Console Help: Spam](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899034). Third-party 2026 guides report that bare TWAs are often rejected for minimum functionality (no offline handling, no app-like features): [testerscommunity.com (2026)](https://www.testerscommunity.com/guides/twa-app-rejected-switch-to-webview), [appconsolelab.com](https://appconsolelab.com/blog/why-is-your-twa-app-repeatedly-rejected-for-google-play-production-access). These are vendors; treat them with caution.
- TWA basics: our site rendered by the user's browser, full screen; ownership proven with Digital Asset Links (`/.well-known/assetlinks.json` with the Play App Signing SHA-256); needs Chrome 72+, otherwise falls back to a Custom Tab with a toolbar. [Chrome for Developers: Trusted Web Activity](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/android/trusted-web-activity/).
- Tools: Bubblewrap CLI builds the `.aab` (latest reported 1.27.0, Oct 2026, targeting Android 16 / API 36, which Play requires for new apps since 31 Aug 2026); PWABuilder's Android package is Bubblewrap underneath. [Bubblewrap on GitHub](https://github.com/GoogleChromeLabs/bubblewrap/tree/main/packages/cli), [MobiLoud (Oct 2026)](https://www.mobiloud.com/blog/publishing-pwa-app-store/), [Google codelab: PWA in Play](https://developers.google.com/codelabs/pwa-in-play). The version numbers come from a third party; not checked on npm.

### Temporary availability

- Unpublish: Test and release › Setup › Advanced settings › App availability › Unpublish. "Existing users can still use your app and receive app updates"; new users can't find or download it. [Play Console Help: Unpublish](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859350).
- Delete: the app must be unpublished, with nothing in review, not rejected or suspended, on <1,000 active devices (on in the last 30 days); not monetized; done by the account owner with the registration transaction ID. Recoverable for 7 days, then permanent; data and metrics are lost; a package name with any installs can never be reused, even by the same account. The page doesn't say what happens to installed copies (third parties: they keep working, with no updates). [Play Console Help: Delete an app](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16483176) (a feature added in 2025; older articles saying "you can't delete an app" are out of date).
- Test tracks: internal up to 100 testers by email; closed by email lists or Google Groups, with an opt-in link and a Google account needed; open testing visible on Play to anyone. Internal and closed test apps don't show in Play search. [Play Console Help: Set up an open, closed or internal test](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9845334).
- Account closure is only for inactivity. [Play Console Help: Closure of inactive developer accounts](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/11605267).

### QR and link capturing

- A WebAPK (a PWA installed from Chrome on Android) registers intent filters for every URL in the manifest `scope`; in-scope links from other apps open the installed app. [web.dev: WebAPKs on Android](https://web.dev/webapks) (an old article), [OpenPWA: WebAPK (Jun 2026)](https://openpwa.net/reference/installation/webapk/).
- Since Android 12, links that aren't verified open in the default browser with no chooser. [Android Developers: Verify App Links](https://developer.android.com/training/app-links/verify-applinks).
- WebAPK claims usually show as verified, but a 2026 Chromium bug repro reports them sometimes becoming "non-verified" after install, and then links stop opening the app. [GitHub repro](https://github.com/brianwang1984-bd/webapk-render-nav-test-pwa). Not an official source.
- Chrome's new "links open the installed app" behaviour (Chrome 139+, `launch_handler`) is documented for Windows, Mac and Linux, not Android. [Chrome for Developers: Navigation management into installed PWAs (19 Aug 2025)](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/capabilities/pwa-navigation-management).
- Google Lens from the Google app often opens a scanned link in its own in-app browser (a Custom Tab), so no app hand-off; the Quick Settings scanner hands the link to Chrome. [Android Central forum](https://forums.androidcentral.com/threads/view-full-url-in-google-lens.1075040/), [Chrome Help community thread](https://support.google.com/chrome/thread/380819582/running-in-chrome-vs-chrome-browser-app?hl=en). Community sources only.
- Storage: a WebAPK and a TWA both run in Chrome and share Chrome's storage for our site, so cards saved in a Chrome tab show in the installed app. If the scan opens another browser (Samsung Internet, Firefox, the Google app), it starts with empty storage. This is my inference from how both work; check it on a real phone.

### Install button

- Criteria: not already installed; HTTPS; manifest with `name`/`short_name`, 192 px and 512 px icons, `start_url`, `display` standalone/fullscreen/minimal-ui; `prefer_related_applications` not true; the user tapped the page at least once and spent ≥30 s on it. [web.dev: Install criteria](https://web.dev/articles/install-criteria).
- No service worker needed for menu install since Chrome 108 on Android (Dec 2023 post). [Chrome for Developers: Revisiting installability criteria](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/update-install-criteria). A third-party test reports `beforeinstallprompt` firing without a service worker in Chrome 153: [progressivewebapps.com (2026)](https://progressivewebapps.com/fundamentals/installability/).
- `preventDefault()` stops the mini-infobar; `prompt()` works once per event; `appinstalled` fires on success; adding `description` and `screenshots` to the manifest gets the larger, store-like install sheet on Android. [web.dev: Installation prompt](https://web.dev/learn/pwa/installation-prompt).
- `getInstalledRelatedApps()` with `{"platform":"webapp","url":"/manifest.webmanifest"}`: Android Chrome 84+, desktop Chrome/Edge 140+ (desktop also needs `id`); call it from inside the scope, otherwise it returns `[]`; only the first 3 entries count. [Chrome for Developers: Get Installed Related Apps](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/capabilities/get-installed-related-apps).
- `navigator.install()` and `<install>`: shipped in Chrome 158 (stable 17 Nov 2026), desktop first; "Android will be supported later". [Chrome 158 release notes](https://chromestatus.com/release-notes/158), [blink-dev Intent to Ship (Sep 2026)](http://www.mail-archive.com/blink-dev@chromium.org/msg17503.html).

## Effort and risks

**PWA path (recommended).** About 1–2 days: the manifest (scope covering `/26/…`, icons, `description`, `screenshots`, `id`, `related_applications`), the button logic, the already-installed states, and a test on 3–4 real Android phones (Pixel and Samsung; camera, Quick Settings tile, Lens; with and without the app installed).
- Risk: the scan opens a different browser than the one holding the saved cards, so the player sees an empty list. Fix: the URL alone must be enough to show that card.
- Risk: an install button confuses some players. Keep it small, secondary and dismissible, never in the way of the card.

**TWA path (not recommended).** About 2–3 days of work (Bubblewrap, `assetlinks.json` served by the Worker, the listing in Catalan, screenshots, a privacy policy, the Data safety form, content rating, target audience), spread over 3–4 weeks of waiting.
- Risk: the calendar. 14 days of testing, then up to 7 days for production access, then the app review, which lands around 18–25 Dec if the test started today.
- Risk: rejection for minimum functionality.
- Risk: we need 12 real testers with Google accounts who stay opted in.
- Risk: the store listing publicly shows a developer name and contact email.
- Risk: the package name is burnt forever.
- Risk: installed copies outlive the event.

## Open points for the user

1. Is the Play account **personal or organisation**, and when was it created? If personal and created after 13 Nov 2023, the TWA can't realistically be ready by 27 Dec. Even if it could, do you still want one, (a) yes as an extra or (b) no, the PWA only?
2. Install button: (a) show our own small "Instal·la l'app" button on Android Chrome when the browser offers it, or (b) no install promotion at all; the page works in the browser and that's enough.
3. Once installed: when the player opens the page in a browser tab, show (a) a hint "Ja tens l'app: obre-la", or (b) nothing.
4. Real-phone check: who has a Samsung and an older Android phone we can test the scan on, before the cards are printed on 4–5 Dec? The printed URL won't change, but the test tells us what the presenters should say.
